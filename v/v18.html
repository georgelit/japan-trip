The plan, day by day

Sunday 14 February → Tuesday 23 February 2027

The mountains-or-coast question is settled, and the answer was "both". Rostik has been to Takayama and says it is not worth a stop, so it is out. He wants Toyama, which was Nadir's original ask. It turns out we never had to choose: Okuhida sits on the road north to Toyama, so the ropeway and the ryokan survive and the coast gets added on top.

Tokyo, Nagano, Hakuba, Okuhida, Toyama, Kanazawa, back to Tokyo. A real circle around the Northern Alps with no backtracking, and Takayama is only ever driven through.

Day 1Sun 14 Feb💝 → departure

Valentine's Day at home in Berlin. This is why the whole trip moved a day later than the first draft: nobody wanted to spend it over Siberia.

Fly out that evening after 21:00. One stop, roughly 15 hours door to door.

Night: on the plane
Day 2Mon 15 Febarrival

Land at Haneda in the evening and keep moving: Hokuriku Shinkansen straight to Nagano, 77–95 minutes, ¥8,000–9,500. Sleep by the station.

It is a rough evening, and it is what buys the third ski day. Zenko-ji is right there if anyone surfaces early.

Night: Nagano
Day 3Tue 16 Feb🎿 🏂 Happo-One

Pick up the car in Nagano, drive to Hakuba, about an hour. On the snow by 10:30.

Happo-One. 52 km of runs, 21 lifts, 1,071 m of vertical, and the slope the 1998 Olympic downhill was raced on. Worth knowing for Nadir and Rostik: despite its reputation as a skiers’ mountain, Happo-One is rated the best resort in Hakuba for snowboarders, because the terrain flows and there are almost no flat sections to skate across.

Rostik is teaching himself, no school booked. His ground is the beginner zones at the base, Nakiyama and Sakka, plus the Shirakaba slope at a maximum of 20°.

Night: Hakuba
Day 4Wed 17 Feb🎿 🏂 Goryu + 47

Goryu and Hakuba47. These two are physically connected by a gondola at the top and share one ticket: 24 runs, 14 lifts and 2 gondolas across three areas, all inside our Hakuba Valley pass anyway.

Good split for our group. Goryu has the gentle terrain for Rostik, and Hakuba47 has what is widely called the best terrain park in Japan, which makes it Nadir’s day.

Night: Hakuba
Day 5Thu 18 Feb🎂 Nadir’s day

Last morning on snow, and it is Nadir’s birthday, so he picks the mountain. Happo-One again, or north up the valley to Cortina and Tsugaike for trees and deep snow. Off the snow around 12:30, because the afternoon is a drive.

Hakuba → Matsumoto → Route 158 through the Abo Tunnel to Okuhida, about 2 h 30. The road has never been closed by snow even in deep winter, but it is a narrow pass with a long winding descent, so no hurry.

The birthday dinner is the ryokan kaiseki, with a rotenburo looking at the mountains. And Thursday is the cheapest night of the week in Japan, 15–30 % under a Saturday.

Night: 🏮 Okuhida ryokan with an open-air rotenburo · the one deliberate splurge
Day 6Fri 19 Feb2,156 m → Toyama

The Shinhotaka Ropeway at 09:00, when it opens, straight from the ryokan. Double-decker gondola to an observation deck at 2,156 m with a 360° view of the Hida range: Yarigatake, Hotaka, Kasagatake, Yakedake. ¥3,300 return, winter hours 09:00–16:15.

This matters more than it sounds. Kamikochi is shut from 16 November to 16 April and the Tateyama Kurobe route does not open until mid-April, so this ropeway is the only way to get close to the Northern Alps in February.

Down by midday, then north to Toyama, about 1 h 30: Route 471 east out of Hirayu, then Route 41. We pass Takayama without stopping. Drop the car in Toyama, the Shinkansen takes over from here.

Afternoon and evening: Toyama Bay sushi, the Kengo Kuma glass museum, Kansui Park.

Night: Toyama
Day 7Sat 20 FebKanazawa

Toyama to Kanazawa is 19 to 22 minutes on the Shinkansen, so this is a short hop and a long day in the city.

Omicho Market, Kanazawa's food market since the Edo period, open 09:00 to 17:00. This is the reason to be here in February: November to February is snow crab season and the local snow crab is rated among the best in Japan. Then Kenrokuen, one of Japan's three great gardens, and the Higashi Chaya geisha quarter.

Night: Kanazawa
Day 8Sun 21 FebKanazawa → Tokyo

Kanazawa in the morning, whatever we did not get to yesterday, then the Kagayaki to Tokyo: 2 h 28, ¥14,380.

Evening in Tokyo, nothing planned yet. This day used to be an empty hole in the plan and Kanazawa filled it.

Night: Tokyo
Day 9Mon 22 Feb🏎 the drive

The whole day is built around one thing. Pick up the car around midday, drive central Tokyo and the busy districts through the evening, drop it back and fly.

Self-drive with OnlyJDM, who we have rented from before. Last time we drove out of Tokyo; this time the point is the city itself. Their Haneda Airport branch is what makes the day a straight line: drop the car, walk to the terminal, board.

They price a 7-hour tier, which is exactly this. An R34 Skyline runs ¥20,800 for those seven hours, about €28 a head split four ways. An R35 GT-R is ¥50,800. The R34 and the EVO seat five, so we all fit in one car, but three of us hold translated licences, so two cars is just as possible.

Night: on the plane
Day 10Tue 23 Febhome

Land in Berlin in the morning. It is the Emperor’s Birthday in Japan that day and we are already gone, which is exactly the point.

7 nights: Nagano ×1, Hakuba ×2, Okuhida ×1, Toyama ×1, Kanazawa ×1, Tokyo ×1

Why the merge works

mountains and coast were never mutually exclusive

What made the two look like an either-or was an assumption that the coast is only reachable from Hakuba along Route 148 through Itoigawa, a known winter bottleneck: narrow, icy, lots of tunnels, slow behind the plough. It is not the only way.

Okuhida is on the road to Toyama. Route 471 east out of Hirayu joins Route 41, and Toyama Interchange to Hirayu is about 90 minutes. So the shape is Hakuba, south into the mountains for the ropeway and the ryokan, then north-west to the sea. One continuous arc, and it sidesteps Route 148 completely.

Why we moved a day: Valentine's Day

Sunday 14 February. Under the first draft it was spent in the air. Georgi and Nadir both wanted it at home instead, so the whole trip shifted one day later and we fly out that evening.

Worth knowing, since it came up: Japan's 14 February is not a date-night occasion at all. It is one-directional chocolate giving, often giri-choco, “obligation chocolate” for coworkers, with the reciprocal falling on White Day, 14 March. So there was nothing to stay for on the Japanese side either.

Things that will bite if we forget them

checked against official sources

A German International Driving Permit is not valid in Japan. Japan recognises the 1949 Geneva Convention, Germany signed the 1968 Vienna one. German licence holders need an official Japanese translation carried together with the original licence. It comes from ADAC Südbayern, the only licensed office in Europe: €65 in person in Munich, €70 by post, 10–14 working days, and they need the physical licence, not a scan.

Three of us already have theirs. Anyone else who wants to drive needs their own.

  • Winter tyres must be booked with the car. Rental companies will not fit them on arrival. Snow tyres or chains are required on Route 158 from mid-November to early April.
  • No car rental exists in Okuhida or Hirayu. Nearest offices are downtown Takayama, Matsumoto or Toyama, which is why we drop in Toyama.
  • No visa for German passports. Visa-free up to 90 days. JESTA, Japan's ESTA equivalent, only starts in fiscal year 2028, so it does not touch this trip.
  • The Shirakawa-go winter light-up is not available to us. In 2027 it runs on four evenings only, all in January: the 11th, 17th, 24th and 31st, advance booking only. The village itself is open and deep in snow in February, just without the illumination.
  • The car is done by day 6. We drop it in Toyama and the Shinkansen takes over, so no mountain road is driven anywhere near the flight day.

Who flies when

two questions, both analysed, neither decided

A. Rostik or Jakob arriving earlier, or staying longer

Easy, and it disturbs nothing, as long as the extra days go at the front.

Why the front and not the back. The group leaves Hakuba on day 5, Thursday 18 February, and does not come back: from there it is Okuhida, Toyama, Kanazawa, Tokyo. Extra days at the back would mean splitting off mid-trip and finding a separate way home. Extra days at the front just means being in Hakuba before the rest of us turn up.

Nothing shared has to change. Hakuba nights are per night, lift passes are per day, and Tokyo → Nagano → Hakuba needs no car: Shinkansen 77–95 min, then the Alpico bus about an hour. Whoever comes early travels independently and we meet on the mountain.
