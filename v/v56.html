Why the merge works

mountains and coast were never mutually exclusive

What made the two look like an either-or was an assumption that the coast is only reachable from Hakuba along Route 148 through Itoigawa, a known winter bottleneck: narrow, icy, lots of tunnels, slow behind the plough. It is not the only way.

Okuhida is on the road to Toyama. Route 471 east out of Hirayu joins Route 41, and Toyama Interchange to Hirayu is about 90 minutes. So the shape is Hakuba, south into the mountains for the ropeway and the ryokan, then north-west to the sea. One continuous arc, and it sidesteps Route 148 completely.

Why exactly these dates

clean window, both ends

14–23 February dodges everything that matters. We miss the Chinese New Year wave (peak 6–13 Feb), both Japanese holidays, and the German school and carnival price spikes. Ski days are Tuesday to Thursday, no weekends. Mid-February is also the deepest snow base of the season: December and January snow has settled, the spring melt has not started.

What to eat, by place

each stop has something you cannot get as well anywhere else

Nagano · Monday night, Tuesday morning

  • Shinshu soba. Nagano is the soba prefecture. Order zaru soba, cold, with dashi, grated daikon and spring onion. Whatever is open near the station at midnight will be a soba or ramen counter.
  • Oyaki. Buckwheat dumplings stuffed with nozawana pickled greens, pumpkin or sweet bean. Station kiosks sell them. Tuesday breakfast, in the car.

Hakuba · three evenings

  • Basashi, horse sashimi. Nagano is one of two places in Japan where this is normal. Thin, with ginger and garlic soy. This is the place to try it.
  • Sanzoku-yaki, a huge marinated fried chicken thigh, the Shinshu pub staple.
  • Shinshu salmon sashimi, a trout-salmon hybrid farmed only here.
  • Asuka in Happo is the izakaya people name first. Hakuba Hifumi does a proper multi-course dinner in a ryokan setting.
  • Shinshu jizake. Nagano is a serious sake prefecture. Ask for a flight.

Okuhida · the ryokan night

We do not order here, the kaiseki comes as a set and that is the point. Expect:

  • Hida beef, the headline, top-grade wagyu from the Hida region. At a good ryokan it comes twice in one meal: lightly seared, and grilled at the table on a magnolia leaf over sweet miso.
  • Hoba miso, that magnolia-leaf miso itself, with mushrooms and spring onion. The dish the region is known for.
  • Salt-grilled river fish, iwana or amago, whole on a skewer. Sansai mountain vegetables as tempura.
  • Dinner runs at a fixed hour and the kitchen does not wait. Tell them when booking that it is a birthday.

Toyama · Friday evening

Toyama Bay drops to 1,000 m close to shore, which is why the seafood is unusual.

  • Shiro-ebi, white shrimp, translucent and sweet, raw as sushi or as a kakiage fritter. Toyama is effectively the only place in Japan that lands them.
  • Masu-zushi, pressed trout sushi in a round wooden box lined with bamboo leaves. Toyama's signature; the station sells it.
  • Buri, winter yellowtail, fattest in February. Sashimi or shabu-shabu.
  • ⚠ Hotaru-ika, firefly squid, is a March to June thing. We will miss the fresh ones.
  • Sushi-Ei Sogawa, central Toyama, from Nadir's map.

Kanazawa · Saturday

The Kaga domain was rich for three centuries and its food is refined rather than rustic.

  • Snow crab. November to February is the season and we are in its last weeks. At Omicho: legs grilled at the stall, or a kaisendon rice bowl for a late breakfast.
  • Nodoguro, blackthroat seaperch, the expensive one: sweet, oily, dense. Grilled with salt or as sushi. If there is one splurge fish on the trip, this is it.
  • Jibuni, duck simmered with wheat gluten in thickened dashi, Kanazawa's classic stew. Evening, at an izakaya.
  • KIRARI at the station's west exit for tablet-ordered sushi. Gold leaf on everything including ice cream: once, for the photo.

Tokyo · Sunday night, Monday

This list is everyone's to build. Mogi knows the city and will have opinions, which is useful, not a job assignment. What we would not want to miss: a proper ramen counter, tsukemen, a standing sushi bar, a yakitori alley. And Daikoku PA itself has a food court that is part of the ritual.

A pattern worth noticing. The loop goes mountains, mountains, sea, sea, city. The food does the same: buckwheat and horse in Nagano, beef and river fish in Hida, white shrimp and yellowtail on the coast, crab and nodoguro in Kanazawa, then whatever Tokyo does best. Nothing repeats.

Things that will bite if we forget them

Driving paperwork is done. Everyone in the group holds what they need to rent and drive in Japan. Nothing to arrange, nothing to confirm at the desk.
