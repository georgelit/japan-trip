The plan, day by day

Saturday 13 February → Monday 22 February 2027

Two shapes are on the table and nobody has chosen yet. Below is route A, the mountains, worked out in full. Route B, the coast, is Nadir's circle through Toyama and Kanazawa and is costed further down. Days 1 to 5 are identical in both.

Route A is a loop around the Northern Alps with no backtracking: Tokyo, Matsumoto, Hakuba, Okuhida, Takayama, then back through Matsumoto to Tokyo. Matsumoto sits on the way in, which is the trick that makes ten days enough.

Day 1Sat 13 Febdeparture

Fly out of Berlin after 21:00. One stop, roughly 15 hours door to door.

Night: on the plane
Day 2Sun 14 Febarrival

Land at Haneda in the evening. Nothing ambitious: eat something, sleep, let the jet lag start unwinding. Haneda is much closer to the centre than Narita.

Night: Tokyo · book early, this is still Chinese New Year week
Day 3Mon 15 Febtransfer + Matsumoto

Limited Express Azusa from Shinjuku to Matsumoto, 2 h 30, ¥6,620. Matsumoto Castle is a sixteenth century original and a designated National Treasure, and the town does very good soba.

Pick up the car in Matsumoto, drive to Hakuba, about an hour. Settle in.

Night: Hakuba (1 of 4)
Day 4Tue 16 Feb🎿 🏂 Happo-One

Happo-One. 52 km of runs, 21 lifts, 1,071 m of vertical, and the slope the 1998 Olympic downhill was raced on. Ride until early afternoon, then the village.

Worth knowing for Nadir and Rostik: despite its reputation as a skiers' mountain, Happo-One is rated the best resort in Hakuba for snowboarders, precisely because the terrain flows and there are almost no flat sections to skate across.

Rostik is teaching himself, no school booked. His ground is the beginner zones at the base, Nakiyama and Sakka, plus the Shirakaba slope at a maximum of 20°.

Night: Hakuba (2 of 4)
Day 5Wed 17 Feb🎿 🏂 Goryu + 47

Goryu and Hakuba47. These two are physically connected by a gondola at the top and share one ticket: 24 runs, 14 lifts and 2 gondolas across three areas, all inside our Hakuba Valley pass anyway.

Good split for our group. Goryu has the gentle terrain for Rostik, and Hakuba47 has what is widely called the best terrain park in Japan, which makes it Nadir's day.

The pass covers ten resorts, 133 km and 109 lifts, but the valley is 40 km long, so it is one or two resorts a day and no more. The car is what makes even that realistic.

Night: Hakuba (3 of 4)
Day 6Thu 18 Feb🎂 Nadir's day

Last morning on snow, and it is Nadir's birthday, so he picks the mountain. Happo-One again, or north up the valley to Cortina and Tsugaike for trees and deep snow. Off the snow around 12:30, because the afternoon is a drive.

Hakuba → Matsumoto → Route 158 through the Abo Tunnel to Okuhida, about 2 h 30. The road has never been closed by snow even in deep winter, but it is a narrow pass with a long winding descent, so no hurry.

The birthday dinner is the ryokan kaiseki, with a rotenburo looking at the mountains. That is the whole reason the day was rebuilt this way.

Night: 🏮 Okuhida ryokan with an open-air rotenburo · the one deliberate splurge
Day 7Fri 19 Feb2,156 m

The Shinhotaka Ropeway at 09:00, when it opens, straight from the ryokan. Double-decker gondola to an observation deck at 2,156 m with a 360° view of the Hida range: Yarigatake, Hotaka, Kasagatake, Yakedake. ¥3,300 return, winter hours 09:00–16:15.

This matters more than it sounds. Kamikochi is shut from 16 November to 16 April and the Tateyama Kurobe route does not open until mid-April, so this ropeway is the only way to get close to the Northern Alps in February.

Down by midday, then on to Takayama, 50 minutes. Sanmachi Suji, the Edo-era merchant quarter, Hida beef, sake breweries.

Night: Takayama
Day 8Sat 20 FebShirakawa-go → Tokyo

Takayama morning market from 08:00, then out to Shirakawa-go, 50 minutes each way. The gassho-zukuri village under snow is the picture everybody knows.

Back through Takayama, drive to Matsumoto over Route 158 (~2 h 30), drop the car, Azusa to Tokyo, in the city in the evening.

Note that the mountain pass is now driven a full day before the flight, not on the morning we fly. That was a real risk in the first draft and it is gone.

Night: Tokyo
Day 9Sun 21 FebTokyo, all of it

A full day in Tokyo, which the Thursday ryokan move bought us. Nothing planned yet, so this is open for whatever the four of us want.

The evening is the natural slot for the JDM night drive if we book it: Rainbow Bridge and Daikoku PA, the actual expressway car-meet spot. It has to end with enough time to reach Haneda for the night flight.

Night: on the plane
Day 10Mon 22 Febhome

Land in Berlin in the morning.

7 nights: Tokyo ×1, Hakuba ×3, Okuhida ×1, Takayama ×1, Tokyo ×1

What route B is actually for

  • February is peak seafood season on this coast. Omicho Market has been Kanazawa's food market since the Edo period, open 09:00–17:00 daily, and November to February is snow crab season, with the local snow crab rated among the best in Japan.
  • Kenrokuen, one of Japan's three great gardens, opens from 06:00 in winter.
  • ⚠️ Kanazawa's winter illuminations run “until mid-February”, so 20 February probably misses them. Not verified for 2027.
  • ⚠️ Reality check on the vibe. Nadir wanted “little Tokyo”. Kanazawa's actual reputation is “Little Kyoto”: preserved geisha and samurai quarters, gardens, crafts. Livelier and more urban than Takayama, but not a night-life city.

🚗 The big practical difference: route B needs no car

All of route B runs on the Hokuriku Shinkansen corridor, and that matters more than it sounds.

  • No one-way drop fee. Picking a car up in Nagano or Matsumoto and dropping it in Kanazawa crosses prefectures. Quoted drop fees run ¥20,000 to over ¥30,000.
  • No Route 148. The Hakuba to Itoigawa road is a known winter bottleneck: narrow, icy, many tunnels, slow behind the plough in heavy snow.
  • The trade is we lose the car, and with it the freedom to hop between Hakuba Valley resorts. Hakuba runs shuttle buses, so that is inconvenience rather than a blocker.

Why exactly these dates

six things to dodge, from two directions

February 2027 starts on a Monday. The dates were not picked for convenience: the first two weeks of February are the worst possible window, hit from both sides at once. Expensive tickets out of Germany, crowded slopes in Japan.

Japan side

6 FebChinese New Year, Year of the Fire GoatWave from China, Hong Kong, Taiwan, Singapore. Japanese resorts are their main winter destination. Peak roughly 6–13 Feb
11 Feb (Thu)National Foundation DayMany take the Friday too, so it becomes a four-day weekend on the slopes

Germany side

So 13–22 February is the clean window. We land on the 14th with the Chinese wave gone and the 11th behind us. We ski Tuesday to Thursday, clean midweek days with no holiday anywhere near them. We fly home on the 22nd, the day before the Emperor's Birthday refills everything. And mid-February is the deepest snow base of the season: December and January snow has settled, the spring melt has not started.

One soft spot: 13–14 February is the tail of Chinese New Year week, so the Tokyo hotel for the night of the 14th should be booked early.

Things that will bite if we forget them

checked against official sources

A German International Driving Permit is not valid in Japan. Japan recognises the 1949 Geneva Convention, Germany signed the 1968 Vienna one. German licence holders need an official Japanese translation carried together with the original licence. It comes from ADAC Südbayern, the only licensed office in Europe: €65 in person in Munich, €70 by post, 10–14 working days, and they need the physical licence, not a scan.

Georgi and Nadir already have theirs. Anyone else who wants to drive needs their own.

  • Winter tyres must be booked with the car. Rental companies will not fit them on arrival. Snow tyres or chains are required on Route 158 from mid-November to early April.
  • No car rental exists in Okuhida or Hirayu. Nearest offices are downtown Takayama or downtown Matsumoto, which is why the car is picked up and dropped in Matsumoto.
  • No visa for German passports. Visa-free up to 90 days. JESTA, Japan's ESTA equivalent, only starts in fiscal year 2028, so it does not touch this trip.
  • The Shirakawa-go winter light-up is not available to us. In 2027 it runs on four evenings only, all in January: the 11th, 17th, 24th and 31st, advance booking only. The village itself is open and deep in snow in February, just without the illumination.
  • Day 9 is the tight one. Route 158 on the morning we fly home. The road does not close, but snow slows it. Leaving Takayama by 09:00 leaves about ten hours of buffer.
